# Rail Frontier game design

Product target: a single-player railroad strategy game in a living model railway landscape. Current deliverable is an interactive 3D Norway architecture study; the complete economic game remains the implementation milestone.

The Norwegian Fjords campaign proves the complete loop before other environments receive production work. The player connects shoreline settlements, earns passenger fares, then builds a timber/lumber chain. Network decisions should balance construction expense, terrain, demand, running costs and future expansion. The original fictional settlements Sundvik, Granli and Fjellhavn are provisional content; their positions must be fitted to the future generated terrain.

First player journey: choose campaign → inspect towns/demand → preview rail and engineering cost → confirm affordable alignment → build two connected stations → buy locomotive and passenger coaches → create shuttle route → observe station dwell/boarding → travel and unloading → receive fare and incur operating costs → expand → save/reload and continue. Every step needs visible feedback and error recovery.

Construction preview shows acceptable, expensive and invalid spans with text/icons as well as color. Show total cost, length, maximum grade and bridge/tunnel breakdown before spending. The implementation certifies 4% maximum grade and 100 m minimum radius conservatively, then applies physical traction and operating costs. Valleyside routes should be longer and cheaper; mountain tunnels shorter and costly. Terrain must drive this choice.

Passenger demand is destination-specific and population-based, regenerates on economic days, and respects capacity. Income occurs only on destination delivery. The first transport system has four cargo types: passengers, mail, timber and lumber. Passenger coaches carry mail in a separate compartment between served settlements. A sawmill consumes timber and produces lumber against local town demand. Every inventory and transfer is inspectable. Operating reports distinguish revenue, running costs and capital expenditure.

Town economy is evaluated once per economic day. A town needs a covered station on an active route to count as connected. Connection raises activity; lumber received during that day raises it further. Activity above the growth threshold adds deterministic population through a saved fractional carry. Lumber demand is capped at 30 days and waiting mail at seven days so an unserved town remains bounded. Mail leaves town stock only when loaded and earns income only when its addressed train lot reaches the destination station.

Visual goal: deep blue water, steep green and rocky slopes, high-altitude snow, dense forest, small original Norwegian-style buildings, moving trains, waterfall mist, bridges and tunnel portals. Strategic camera must smoothly pan, zoom, rotate and tilt, focus selected objects and follow trains. Tool cancellation and strategic-view reset must be accessible by keyboard.

Station size progresses from rural halt through small, town and city stations to freight and major terminals. Larger classes cost more to build and maintain while increasing catchment, storage and usable train length. Existing stations can be expanded for the price difference; routes must fit their shortest platform.

The UI includes a main menu (new/continue/load/settings/credits), cash/date/speed/objective HUD, primary rail/station/train/route tools, contextual selection panels and live finance reports. Avoid covering the world with permanent panels. The forest-green field-survey UI uses warm pale type and brass accents, locally bundled DM Sans/Libre Caslon Display, a lightweight DOM layer and Three.js/Vite. Additional campaigns and deeper management panels remain planned.

Deferred: competitors, multiplayer, stocks, advanced signals, the full vehicle catalogue and full weather physics. The campaign calendar and data-driven purchase boundary are implemented. Norway begins with one original early-era steam locomotive, passenger coach and freight wagon; the heavier, faster and cheaper-running Nord Di 3B enters in 1960 at a higher capital price. Researched electric models require route electrification before their Blender assets can be enabled.
