## Timber houses: colour, construction and materials

Most residential structures should read as wood. The three house geometries from GFX-006 each receive the four required colour families. The following sRGB values are **chosen art starting points**, not sampled paint specifications or historically measured pigments.

| Family | Wall base | Trim / accents | Required visible character |
|---|---|---|---|
| Iron-oxide red | `#9E4034` | Warm white `#EEE8DC`; dark roof | Painted boards retain grain, slight board-to-board variation and restrained lower-wall weathering. |
| Ochre yellow | `#C49A4C` | Warm white; occasional darker door | Earthy yellow with visible seams, avoiding saturated plastic yellow. |
| Charcoal / brown-black | `#302D29` | White window surrounds; either dark or white verge boards | Preserve grain and reflected daylight in shadows; never use featureless absolute black. T2 supports the dark-edge variant. |
| Warm white | `#E5E0D5` | White/light-grey trim; red, muted blue or dark door | Separate glass, trim relief and siding roughness so walls remain readable in sunlight. |
| Weathered unpainted timber | Grey-brown variation | Timber or restrained painted details | Secondary treatment for sheds, barns and boathouses; not a substitute for the four requested house colours. |

Deliver at least **eight curated wall/trim/door combinations** across the three homes: red/white, red/dark, ochre/white, ochre/cream, charcoal/white verge, charcoal/dark verge with white windows, white/grey with red door, white/white with dark door. White trim must visibly trace selected gable verges, eaves/fascia, corner boards and window frames. Some houses deliberately retain dark roof edges. Avoid independently randomising every component into implausible combinations.

Choose variants deterministically from stable settlement/building IDs, with village-specific weights and limited repeats on adjacent plots. Include red, yellow, black and white examples in the acceptance village view. Colour proportions are composition choices, not a claimed historical distribution. Keep roof material, foundations and glass independent of wall tint.

- Author horizontal overlapping boards and vertical board-and-batten variants with coherent orientation, approximately 0.12–0.22 m board widths as a starting range. Tile grain/seams in physical units, rather than stretching one facade bitmap across each differently sized house.
- Geometry carries silhouette and parallax: roof overhang, verge/fascia boards, corner trim, window/door recesses, sills, porch posts, chimney and stone foundation. Near LOD has projecting battens where noticeable. Normal maps carry fine grain, small board edges, nails and shallow paint wear. Bake these down consistently for far LOD.
- Roofs use slate-course, restrained tile or documented period-appropriate timber treatments; use slate as the main T1/T2-inspired material. Do not give every roof vivid terracotta, glossy metal or blanket green moss. Moss/dampness favours sheltered edges and ground contact.
- Window panes have divided frames, darker interiors and subtle environment response. Begin with opaque PBR glass over shallow dark recesses to avoid whole-village transparency sorting; no bright cyan rectangles or static photographic reflections. Doors show panels/planks, handle and threshold at close range.
- Separate material regions for wall, trim, door, roof, foundation and glass. Share texture objects and batch compatible regions by palette. Do not tint an entire GLB through one house colour: it would recolour roof, masonry and glass. Start with ordinary material/instance colours per region; introduce a channel-mask shader only if measured draw calls require it, with engine and LOD tests.

### Geometry versus texture on rolling stock

| Detail | Near LOD | Simplified LOD |
|---|---|---|
| Steam pipework, rails, cylinder connections | Low-sided curved tubes with attachment points and plausible connected routes; major pipes cast shadows and remain distinct from boiler. | Retain a few characteristic large pipes; bake fine tubing/fasteners into normal/albedo maps. |
| Rods, wheel rims/spokes, buffers and steps | Geometry; preserve wheel/coupler positions and existing motion contract. Check front orientation against `forward_probe`. | Simplify spokes/steps but preserve axle count and overall silhouette. |
| Front windows, cab doors, large louvres | Recessed glazing/frames, door opening or panel and shallow grille cavity; thin wipers/handles where readable. | Dark inset areas, seals/door seams and grille normal texture; no new silhouette jump. |
| Diesel fans / electric pantographs | Fan housing and roof silhouettes; pantographs, insulators and major conductors use geometry. | Simplified housings/pantograph silhouette; fine fins/conductors baked or omitted by distance. |
| Rivets, small panel seams, fine cooling fins | Normal/roughness/base colour; only enlarge to geometry when visible at the actual follow-camera distance. | Filtered map detail without crawling/moire. |
| Numbers, warning marks, small fittings | Original atlas markings, unmirrored UV islands; only real details for the selected era. | Keep only markings large enough to survive mipmapping. |

Passenger coaches must receive distinct front/end and side doors, window frames/divisions, underframe detail, roof seams and period-oriented wood/paint surfaces. Keep the existing 48-passenger definition and length: a fictional window layout must not silently change capacity. Freight wagons need deck planks, frame/bolts, stakes or side structure matching the current model, buffers and metal-versus-wood roughness. Neither wagon gets copied modern commuter-door graphics. Timber/lumber cargo visibility continues to follow existing cargo rules.

## Reproducible Blender and export specification

Use local Blender 4.0.2 and the established source axes/units/marker contract. Keep separate scenery and rolling-stock generators; share a small material/UV/baking helper. Source procedural graphs, palette recipes, UV layout and bake settings are authoritative. Export original runtime textures and two LOD GLBs; retain editable `.blend` intermediates in the existing ignored source directory.

1. Build the material palette/contact sheet before exporting the full village: all four house colours under neutral daylight, with wall/trim/roof/glass isolated. Add a steam boiler/pipe/cab material sample and annotated future El 1/Di 3/Di 4/El 18 reference briefs.
2. UV unwrap with consistent texel scale: begin at 128–256 px/m for repeating siding, 256–512 px/m for near window/door/locomotive feature islands, using tiled surfaces and trim strips where area exceeds atlas capacity. Validate at the real camera; these are tuning values, not a requirement to create huge atlases.
3. Bake base colour, tangent-space normal and packed ORM (R occlusion, G roughness, B metalness). Colour maps are sRGB; ORM/normal are linear. Painted wood and painted metal are dielectric; exposed metal gets a separate metalness mask. Keep lighting/shadows out of base colour and avoid double-darkening creases through both albedo and AO.
4. Start with 1024² architecture maps and shared roof/trim regions. Allow a 2048² locomotive colour atlas for legible front/side detail, with 1024² normal/ORM until close evidence justifies more. Use UV padding/dilation, initially 8 pixels at 1024 and 16 at 2048, and test distant mips for trim/colour bleed. Geometry and maps must retain compatible normals/UVs across both LODs.
5. Extend the manifest and validator for unique external texture references, map roles/colour space, dimensions and relative-path resolution. GLB shared-image URIs must resolve in both dev and built output. Share loaded texture objects; don't embed duplicate atlas bytes in every building or locomotive. Texture disposal belongs to the asset-library owner, not each train or house.
6. Keep original locomotive limits of ≤12,000 close / ≤3,000 simplified triangles as the initial gate. Account for new material primitives in draw-call measurements. Simplify invisible pipe/fin detail before increasing budgets. Retain current vehicle IDs, bounds, coupler anchors, mass, power, capacity and prices.
7. Record appearance recipes in visual content metadata (asset, palette/livery ID, source IDs, depicted unit, known date intervals and unknowns). For current stock use a fixed compatible appearance; scenery variants derive from stable IDs. Future per-purchase liveries need an explicit persistence decision in their vehicle task; do not add speculative save fields in this pass.

Texture residency must remain within the main plan's **128 MiB decoded target**. A 2048² RGBA8 texture with mipmaps costs approximately 21.3 MiB; 1024² costs 5.3 MiB. Thus a 2K colour plus two 1K locomotive maps starts near 32 MiB; a three-map 1K architecture set near 16 MiB; a shared three-map 1K coach/freight set near 16 MiB. That leaves roughly 64 MiB for terrain, foliage, rock, environment and any extra masks. Count actual unique allocations, not compressed PNG download size. Future-era packs load on demand instead of keeping every future locomotive atlas resident. Compression is optional after a real supported import/budget check; do not assume it solves residency automatically.
